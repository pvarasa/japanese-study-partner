Analysis

Headline finding

qwen3.5:9b is the sweet spot for this app. At 0.61 s warm latency and 96 % accuracy, it gets within rounding of Claude's quality (100 %) at less than half its latency, runs entirely locally, and costs nothing per call. Two of its three "non-good" cases were also non-good on Claude's stricter rubric (very hard idioms like 渋い-face).

Pareto frontier (speed × accuracy)

Each row below is on the frontier — no other model is both faster and more accurate.

Where the local models lose to Claude

The 6 cases where every smaller local model stumbled all share one thing: context‑sensitive sense disambiguation.

  • 結構 in "結構です、ありがとう" — only Claude, qwen3.5:9b, qwen3.6, GLM-4.7-Flash got the polite-refusal sense; qwen2.5 said "pretty good" and qwen3.5:2b gave the dictionary entry "structure, form."
  • 渋い顔 — the "displeased / sour expression" idiom. Most models gave "serious / stern" — close but missing the negative connotation.
  • せっかく — the famously untranslatable "after going to the trouble of." qwen3.5:2b said "fortunately" (opposite); the rest hedged.
  • やばい meaning "amazingly" rather than "dangerous" — modern slang inversion only Claude / qwen3.6 / GLM nailed.
  • ちょっと as a discourse hedge ("ちょっと、それはどうかな…") rather than "a little" — Claude, qwen3.6, TranslateGemma got it; the smaller Qwens gave the dictionary sense.
  • お邪魔します / お世話になっております — Japanese social-set-phrases with conventional English equivalents. qwen2.5 botched both ("I'll be brief", "my pleasure to serve you").

For a JLPT-N3 study tool these are also the most useful cases to translate. So the headline accuracy gap matters more than a flat 4 % suggests.

Cold-load reality check

The bench measured Ollama cold loads after eviction:

  • 2 B: 28 s · 4 B: 96 s · 9 B: 191 s — all tractable; keep_alive: 30m in our request hides this from the user during a study session.
  • 12 B (TranslateGemma): 240 s · 19 GB GLM-4.7-Flash: 508 s · 24 GB qwen3.6: 493 s — these turn the first lookup of a session into a UX disaster unless you pin them with keep_alive permanently.

Caveat: qwen2.5:7b's cold-load reading of 1.4 s in this run is not a fair number — the model was already warm in VRAM from earlier tests. Its real cold load is ~16–25 s based on prior runs.

Notes & caveats

  • Reading field: every local model left the hiragana reading field blank for kanji words; only Claude reliably filled it. The app's existing fugashi server-side fallback in furigana.py:reading_for() handles this — switching to a local model loses no functionality.
  • Reasoning models: qwen3.6 (and presumably any future qwen3.x "thinking" model) needs "think": false in the Ollama request, otherwise it emits a long reasoning preamble that breaks JSON output and ~10× the latency. Already wired into backend/app/translation.py.
